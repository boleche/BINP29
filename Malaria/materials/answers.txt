4. Compare the genome sizes with other eukaryotes and bacteria.
Discuss with your partner (that is student partner) the reason
for the observed genome sizes.

The plasodium genome sizes are much smaller than typical eukaryotes. The toxoplasma gondii genome is larger than the plasmodium species but is still relatively small.
The cause of this is likely because these are parasitic species that require a host cell to function, and therefore do not need as many internal genomic mechanisms to survive.
Toxoplasma gondii is capable of infecting a wide range of hosts, this ability is reflected in its larger genome size.

5. What may cause the biased GC-contents in some of the species?

This could be their specific different abilities to infect different hosts. As genes were selected against in each species to better infect their host, this could cause the GC bias.

9. How many of the BUSCOs are found in all eight organisms?

When you only look at complete BUSCOs: only 1 BUSCO is found in all 8 organisms. 
If you look at complete and duplicated you can see 154 shared between all 8 organisms.

10. If Toxoplasma is removed, how many BUSCOs are shared among the remaining seven species. Interpret!

If Tg is removed you can see 182 shared BUSCOs. This makes sense given our knowledge that Tg is an outgroup.
Especially when you compare complete only (1 busco shared vs. ~170 shared). This difference reflects evolutionary divergence.
The 154 shared reflects conserved genes.
